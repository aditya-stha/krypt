01Brand Strategy — the foundation

A glance should belong to the person who casts it.

Most of the category sells fear: hackers, snoops, the stranger reading over your shoulder. KRYPT sells the opposite — the calm, ordinary dignity of looking at your own screen without being looked at. That single belief drives every decision below.

Purpose · why we exist

Beyond making money

The screen is the most intimate window into a person's life — their messages, money, photographs, half-formed thoughts. The world has quietly turned every one of those screens into a billboard for whoever sits beside us. KRYPT exists to give that private space back. We are not in the business of selling glass; we are in the business of defending the small, daily right to look without being seen.

Mission · the change today

Make privacy the default texture of personal tech

Privacy today is sold as armour — black, defensive, a little paranoid. Our mission is to make it something people actually want, not just tolerate: glass so optically pure that protecting your screen never costs you clarity, and concealment so well-designed that owning your privacy feels like good taste rather than self-defence.

Vision · 5–10 years

"Krypt" becomes a personal privacy standard

We want people to reach for KRYPT the way they reach for a good case — assumed, trusted, a little coveted. Glass is the beginning. The horizon is a small, coherent family of objects that share one idea: privacy you can see and feel, designed like furniture rather than gear. We picture a community that treats its own attention as something worth protecting — and treats protection as something worth designing well.

Core values

How each one shows up in real work

VALUE 01Privacy is a posture, not a panic

We never sell through surveillance horror or fear — we sell through dignity and design. In practice, every ad, box, and headline frames privacy as calm and confident, and we kill any creative that leans on a "someone is watching you" scare.

VALUE 02Clarity is non-negotiable

A privacy layer that dims, distorts, or rainbows the screen has failed, full stop. Every product ships only after passing the internal optical bar — "would we run this on our own phones for a month?" — and we publish our real light-transmittance and haze numbers instead of hiding them.

VALUE 03Make it decor, not gear

The category defaults to black plastic and tactical cues; we refuse them. Every product, color, and insert is designed to look at home on a linen-topped desk or a marble shelf, and our color system is built to be coordinated with a room — not a gadget bag.

VALUE 04Honest by construction

We'd rather under-claim and over-deliver than market our way past the truth. Install guides admit what's genuinely fiddly, support tells a customer when a cheaper fix will do, and we never retouch the privacy effect to look stronger than the physics allows.

Value proposition

KRYPT makes privacy you'd actually want on display: premium anti-reflective and privacy glass engineered for true, undimmed clarity — and built to sit as beautifully on your desk as it does on your phone.

Why the audience cares

Our people — iPhone and high-end Android owners, tech-fluent and taste-driven — already pay for things that are quietly excellent. They don't want a cheaper film; they want the version that respects both their eyes and their shelf. KRYPT is the only privacy glass that treats clarity and design as non-negotiable at the same time.

02Personality & Verbal Identity — the voice

Quietly exacting. We let the product do the shouting — by not shouting.

KRYPT speaks like a person with real taste and nothing to prove: precise, warm, and never anxious. If a sentence feels like it's trying too hard, it isn't ours.

Personality traits

Four words, each with the line it must never cross

TRAIT 01QUIETLY EXACTING

We care about the half-millimetre and the exact word, and it shows in how specific we are.
We are precise, but not preachy — we demonstrate the detail, we don't lecture about it.

TRAIT 02WARMLY MINIMAL

We edit ruthlessly down to what matters, then leave the warmth in.
We are spare, but not cold — minimal is a kindness to the reader, never a pose.

TRAIT 03DISCREETLY CONFIDENT

We know the product is very good, so we can afford to be calm about it.
We are assured, but not boastful — confidence is shown in restraint, not superlatives.

TRAIT 04CALMLY PROTECTIVE

We are unmistakably on the customer's side, guarding what's theirs.
We are protective, but not paranoid — we offer safety, never sell anxiety.

03Visual Identity — the aesthetic

Built like home decor, engineered like an instrument.

Warm neutrals you'd choose for a room, the discipline of an architect's drawing, and exactly one warm spark — reserved entirely for the moment we ask you to act.

Logo

Wordmark · monogram · rules

KRYPT
Clear space = cap-height of the “K”, all sides

Construction

The wordmark is KRYPT set in our display sans, Light weight, in capitals with generous tracking (+0.30–0.34em) — it should read like the name embossed on good stationery, not a tech logotype. The monogram “K”, optically centered, serves app icons and favicons.

Minimum size & placement

Never below 16px digital cap-height (≈14mm print) or the tracking collapses. Prefer top-left or centered, always with at least one cap-height of air around it. Let it breathe — crowding the mark is the most common mistake.

Critical “do nots”

DON’T STRETCHKRYPT
DON’T USE AMBERKRYPT
DON’T ON BUSY ARTKRYPT
DON’T ADD EFFECTSKRYPT

Also never: condense or re-letterspace, recolor outside Ink / Bone / a single approved neutral, fill the mark with the amber accent, apply gradients, shadows, outlines or bevels, rotate it, or rebuild it in another typeface.

Color — accent & balance

One spark, strict ratio

Roughly 60% bone & beige, 30% graphite/ink for text and structure, ~8% product color in context, and under 2% amber. If amber appears more than once on a screen, something is wrong.

Imagery & art direction

Warm daylight · real materials · honest optics

Shoot like this

Soft directional daylight — window light, late afternoon. The phone photographed as an object among home and office things: oak, linen, travertine, plaster, a stoneware mug, a paperback, a pair of glasses. People shown candid and relaxed — hands and posture over faces, privacy as everyday calm (a train, a café, a shared desk). Show the privacy effect honestly: one scene, two angles, crisp head-on and naturally darkened from the side. Generous negative space; one hero object per frame, placed off-center.

Never this

Sterile blue-lit "tech" stock. Glowing circuit boards, HUDs, padlock clipart, binary-code rain. Hooded-hacker imagery or any fear cue. Hard studio-black product voids, lens flares, neon. And never fake the privacy effect to look darker or stronger than the glass actually performs — honesty is part of the picture.

Customer experience philosophy

Handled by someone with taste and no agenda

The whole arc should feel like being looked after by an expert who isn't trying to sell you anything more. Pre-purchase is honest comparison — real numbers, no fake countdowns, no dark patterns. The box itself coaches the install (dust stickers, an alignment frame, a quiet "it's fine to lift and retry"). Afterward we check in and replace a bubbled fit, free. Support resolves before it explains policy.

North star: every interaction should feel like a small, quiet upgrade to the customer's day — never a transaction that demands their attention. The feeling we engineer for, in three words: these people respect me.

Employer brand

We hire for taste, honesty, and craft — over hype

Internally the values become operating rules. Designers get the time to sweat the box, not just the ad. Engineers are empowered to kill a product that misses the optical bar even when it's ready to ship. Support is measured on resolution and warmth, not call time.

Hiring favors work-samples over pedigree — "show us one thing you made better than it needed to be." Job posts are written in brand voice: calm, specific, no "rockstar ninja." And we celebrate the person who caught the haze defect as loudly as the one who shipped the launch. The studio itself should look like the brand: neutral, warm, considered.
